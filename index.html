01 · The premise

The model was never the constraint.

The agent ecosystem is very good and improves monthly. The open-source world has built the locks: sandboxes, permission tooling, audit frameworks. What nobody sells is the adult in the room — someone accountable for what an agent is allowed to touch, what it must ask before doing, and what exactly happened after it acted. That accountability is the whole company. The harness is the product. The model is a line item.

The trap for operators is not agents that fail. It is agents that succeed until you become the bottleneck: dozens of approvals a day routed to your phone because nothing in the system distinguishes a small parts order from wiring a deposit. Approval fatigue turns oversight into a rubber stamp. The fix is structural, not more attention.

02 · Operating principles

Three rules, enforced in code.

01

Harness over model

The model is replaceable and improves on someone else's schedule. The harness — permissions, gates, receipts, the audit trail — is what we build and what you are buying. When a better model ships, your governance does not reset.

02

Least privilege

An agent gets exactly the access its job requires, for as long as the duty exists. No standing broad grants, no just-in-case credentials. When the duty ends, the access ends.

03

Human-in-the-loop, tiered by reversibility

Blast radius decides the gate, not the vendor's comfort. Read-only work runs free. Reversible work runs on its own and leaves a receipt carrying its own rollback. Irreversible work — payments, external sends, anything public — drafts and holds for a human. You are the circuit breaker, and only for the actions that deserve you.

03 · In practice

If the gate is not code, it is a suggestion.

Constraints get written in plain English and compiled into gates the agent cannot talk its way around. What that looks like on a Tuesday:

The send waits

A customer email gets drafted in full. The send holds for you. You release it, or you fix it once and the correction lands in the harness — not in a chat log that resets tomorrow.

Money needs an arm, not a vibe

A payment is staged with the exact amounts shown. Nothing posts until you arm that specific entry. The receipt states the rollback before you approve, not after something goes wrong.

Receipts, not recaps

Every autonomous action writes a receipt: which agent, what tier, what it touched, how to reverse it. The audit trail is generated by the architecture — not reconstructed for the auditor.

Corrections are structural

When something is wrong, the gate changes — not just the output. A fixed mistake cannot quietly come back, and fixes carry forward into every deployment that follows.

Something we got wrong

We once had a tenant health check report "healthy" while its agent had zero working tools — running, and quietly useless. A monitor that cannot tell those apart certifies the failure instead of catching it. Liveness now requires the governed surface to be up, not just the port. Finding and closing that class of gap is most of the job, which is why we publish the cuts instead of the highlight reel.

05 · If you heard about Stan

We retired our own agent. That is the pitch.

Stan was our first full-time agent: his own machine, his own email address, his own duties. People met him. In August we audited ourselves the way we audit clients, and his standing access had outlived his named duties — so we retired him and revoked the access the same day.

The next always-on agent gets admitted when a specific duty exists, not before, because an idle agent with broad access is exactly the liability we are paid to remove. What Stan learned stayed. The access did not. If we will not carry that risk on our own books, we will not deploy it on yours.
